MedR-Bench
Scoring the reasoning process, step by step

Full paper: “Quantifying the reasoning abilities of LLMs on clinical cases” · Qiu, Wu … Yanfeng Wang, Weidi Xie (Shanghai Jiao Tong) · Nature Communications, 2025

Most benchmarks grade the final answer. MedR-Bench grades the reasoning itself — across the whole care journey (examinations → diagnosis → treatment) — with an automated Reasoning Evaluator that breaks a free-text rationale into steps and scores each on three independent axes: efficiency, factuality, completeness.

The frame

Evaluate the whole care trajectory, not one answer

Each case is played out over three stages — the same sequence a clinician moves through. Final outputs are scored with standard metrics; the reasoning behind them is scored separately.

Stage 1

Examination recommendation

Given the presentation (minus test results), recommend the right work-up — 1-turn or free-turn (agent plays the patient, returns results). Scored by precision & recall of tests.

Stage 2

Diagnostic decision-making

Give the diagnosis, with test info at three levels: after 1-turn, after free-turn, or oracle (all results given). Scored by diagnosis accuracy.

Stage 3

Treatment planning

Produce a management plan (oracle setting), incl. a rare-disease subset. Scored by treatment accuracy.

The engine

The Reasoning Evaluator: structure → verify

An agentic scorer that (1) structures the free-text reasoning into discrete steps, then (2) verifies each step against references — online medical search plus the case's ground-truth. It emits three independent metrics:

Efficiency

Efficiency

"How many steps bring extra insight?"

Penalizes redundant / padding steps. Concise, relevant reasoning scores high.

Factuality

Factuality

"How many steps follow medical priors?"

Penalizes steps that contradict medical knowledge — i.e. hallucinated reasoning.

Completeness

Completeness

"How many ground-truth steps are recalled?"

Penalizes missing critical steps — the reasoning the case actually required.

The three are independent — a model can be factual but incomplete, or complete but inefficient. That decoupling is the whole point: "accuracy" hides all of it.

What they found

Right answers, incomplete reasoning

Diagnosis

>85% — when given all the tests

In the oracle setting models diagnose well. But that's the easy part — the info is handed to them.

Examination rec

Recall ~38–44%

Models miss over half the needed work-up. Free-turn doesn't help — they enter repetitive query loops or stop too early. Info-gathering is the weak link.

Reasoning quality

Factual (~87%) but incomplete

Reasoning mostly follows medical priors, yet critical steps are often missing. Missing exam info → more hallucinated reasoning (factuality drops).
