Med-PRM
Grading the reasoning, not just the answer

Full paper: “Med-PRM: Medical Reasoning Models with Stepwise, Guideline-verified Process Rewards” · Yun, Sohn, Park et al. (Korea Univ · ETH Zürich · Yale) · arXiv 2025 · Med-PRM.github.io

The first two papers scored the final response. Neither could tell us which step of the reasoning went wrong, or why. Med-PRM does exactly that: it verifies each reasoning step against retrieved clinical guidelines and labels it correct or incorrect — a process reward model that aligns with expert physicians. This is the missing "reasoning-process accuracy" lens CAMRE needs.

Why it exists

A right answer can hide broken reasoning — and a wrong answer can hide good reasoning

Prior reward models label a step by whether it leads to the correct final answer. Med-PRM argues that's two mistakes waiting to happen — and that medical reasoning specifically needs external knowledge to judge correctly.

Gap 1 · Outcome-driven labels

Valid early steps get punished

Outcome reward models (ORM) and MCTS-style PRMs score a step by the probability it reaches the right answer. So a logically sound early step that happens to end in a wrong answer is labeled "bad" — distorting the signal about what good reasoning looks like.

Gap 2 · No medical grounding

Labels without evidence learn nothing

Judging a clinical step needs knowledge that isn't fully in the model's weights. Training a reward model on bare labels — with no clinical context — can't teach the rationale behind them, and invites hallucination.

The core distinction

Outcome reward vs process reward

One number for the whole trace, versus one number per step. Med-PRM adds a third ingredient the others lack: retrieved guideline evidence as input to the judge.

ORM · Outcome Reward Model

One score for the entire trace

whole trace → single score rS ∈ [0,1]

Correct-or-not for the whole chain, based on the final answer. Cannot localize where reasoning broke.

PRM · Process Reward Model

One score per step

+ Step 1 → rs1
+ Step 2 → rs2
− Step 3 → rs3  ← error localized here

Each step gets a score via the softmax on a +/− token. Med-PRM feeds the judge retrieved documents D too: RM(D, q, S).

How a whole trace is scored: a trace's final score = the minimum reward across its steps (a chain is only as strong as its weakest link). Best-of-N generates several candidate traces and keeps the highest-scoring one — so any trace with even one bad step scores low and gets passed over in favour of a cleaner trace.

How the labels are made

RAG-as-a-Judge, then train a verifier

The four-stage pipeline (Fig 1). The crux is stage 2: labels come from a large LLM judging each step against retrieved evidence, not from whether completions happen to reach the right answer.

STAGE 1

Generate + retrieve

An LLM produces reasoning traces for medical questions; relevant documents are retrieved from guideline/literature corpora.

STAGE 2

Label (RAG-as-a-Judge)

A large LLM assigns a +/− to each step, conditioned on the question and retrieved docs. Validated against experts.

STAGE 3

Train Med-PRM

The labeled traces train the reward model (Llama-3.1-8B). Its input always includes the retrieved documents D.

STAGE 4

Use it

At inference as a best-of-N verifier, or to bootstrap a stronger policy model on its highest-scoring traces.

Why RAG-as-a-Judge beats the alternatives (Fig 3)

ORM labeling

Labels the whole trace by the final answer → marks valid early steps as bad when the answer is wrong.

Rule-based / auto-completion

Labels a step by whether completions from it reach the answer → same outcome bias, and no medical grounding.

RAG-as-a-Judge

Judges each step against retrieved evidence → aligns with expert annotations on both logic and facts.

What it bought

Small models, big reasoning gains

Test-time verifier

+ up to 13.50%

Used as a best-of-N verifier, Med-PRM lifts base-model accuracy by up to 13.5% — no retraining of the base model.

Plug-and-play

80.35% on MedQA

Paired with Meerkat-8B, the first time an 8B-scale model passed 80% on MedQA. Med-PRM is a drop-in component.

vs other PRMs

+3.44% average

Beats conventional PRM baselines across seven benchmarks; SOTA on six of seven.

On open-ended diagnostic tasks (no multiple-choice options), the common trick of majority-voting over answers can't be used — there are no discrete options to tally votes on. Med-PRM's verifier instead scores each candidate reasoning trace and keeps the most clinically plausible one. That open-ended, no-answer-key setting is the one closest to real cardiometabolic decision support.
